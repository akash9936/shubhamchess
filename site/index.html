Levels

Three levels, one learning map

The free assessment class decides where a child starts. Each level is a fixed list of topics taught in order, so you always know what comes next and what has been covered.

♟︎

Beginner

For a child who has never played, or knows only some of the moves.

  • How each piece moves and what it is worth
  • Check, checkmate, stalemate and the rules of a game
  • Reading and writing moves in notation
  • First checkmates: king and queen, king and rook, back-rank mate
  • Opening principles: centre, development, castling and king safety
  • Simple tactics: the fork and the pin
Placed by the free assessmentGroup, pair or one-to-one
♞︎

Intermediate

For a child who plays full games and wants to stop losing pieces.

  • Tactics: double attack, skewer, discovered attack, X-ray attack
  • Counting attackers against defenders before every capture
  • Pawn play: isolated pawns, passed pawns, pawn structure
  • Endgames: king and pawn, opposition, Lucena and Philidor positions
  • One opening for White and one for Black, understood rather than memorised
  • Calculating three moves deep without touching a piece
Placed by the free assessmentGroup, pair or one-to-one
♛︎

Tournament

For a child who plays in school or rated events and wants results.

  • Attacking patterns: Greek Gift sacrifice, smothered mate, deflection
  • Positional play: weak squares, outposts, planning in the middlegame
  • Opening preparation against specific opponents
  • Rook endgames and converting a winning position
  • Review of the student's own tournament games, every week
  • Clock handling, pre-round routine and handling a loss
Placed by the free assessmentUsually one-to-one

How a class runs

One class, in four moves

Every class follows the same order, so students know what to expect and parents know what was covered.

  1. Warm-up puzzles

    Five to ten minutes of short tactics on the theme from last week, to see what stuck.

  2. One new idea

    A single concept taught on the live board with model games. One idea per class, taught properly.

  3. Play it out, then analyse

    Students play positions built around that idea against each other or the coach, say their plan out loud, and go over the game together afterwards.

  4. Weekly homework and assessment

    Puzzle practice to solve before the next class, a regular assessment of progress, and a short message to parents on what to practise. The aim is a better rating and better game sense.

Questions parents ask

Before You Book

What do we need for an online chess class?

A laptop or tablet with a camera, a stable internet connection, and a quiet place to learn. Classes are conducted through Zoom or Google Meet. We use platforms like Lichess and Chess.com for practice and interactive learning.

My child has never played chess. Is that fine?

Absolutely! Beginners are welcome. We start with the basics, including piece movements, chess rules, and simple tactics, and gradually build their skills.

What happens if we miss a class?

Please inform me at least 3–4 hours before the scheduled class if your child cannot attend. Make-up classes can be discussed based on availability. Last-minute cancellations and no-shows may not qualify for a make-up class.

What ages do you teach?

I teach children aged 4–15, from complete beginners to intermediate-level players. Classes are available in English and Hindi.

We live outside India. Can our child join?

Absolutely! I welcome students from around the world. Classes are conducted online, and timings can be arranged according to mutual availability across different time zones.

How are students grouped?

Students are grouped according to their age, playing strength, and learning level to ensure an effective learning experience. Small groups have a maximum of 5 students.

What are the fees?

Per student, starting from ₹1,500 a month for a small group, ₹3,000 for a pair and ₹4,500 for one-to-one. The exact fee depends on the plan length and is shared in the free assessment class, which is not charged.

Do you provide learning materials?

Yes! Students receive practice puzzles, worksheets, lesson materials, and homework to reinforce what they learn in class.
