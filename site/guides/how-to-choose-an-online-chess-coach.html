How to choose an online chess coach for your child

By Shubham Singh, Arena International Master and FIDE rated coach. Updated 9 November 2026.

There are hundreds of online chess coaches, and most of their websites say the same things. These seven checks separate the ones worth a month's fee from the rest.

1. Can you verify the credentials?

Every FIDE rated player has a public profile at ratings.fide.com with their rating and history. If a coach lists a title or rating, ask for the FIDE ID and look it up. A coach who hesitates is telling you something. For the record, this academy's coach is FIDE ID 25799363.

2. How many children in a group?

Online, five is the practical maximum. Beyond that, a child can go a whole class without being asked a question. Ask for the actual number, not "small".

3. How long is a class, and how often?

For children under ten, 45 to 50 minutes twice a week beats one long weekend class. Attention fades, and two touchpoints a week keep the ideas fresh.

4. Is there homework, and does anyone check it?

Chess improvement happens between classes. A good coach sets puzzles each week and looks at them. Ask what happens if the homework is not done, and what the coach does when a child is stuck on it.

5. What does the coach do with a child who is struggling?

Ask this directly. You want to hear about going back a step, changing the explanation, or a quiet word with the parent. You do not want to hear "they will catch up" with nothing behind it.

6. Does the coach still play?

A coach who plays tournaments remembers what pressure feels like and can prepare a child for it. It also means their own understanding is current.

7. Is the first class free, and is it a real class?

A free assessment class should be a lesson with puzzles and a game, ending in a frank recommendation. If the "free demo" is a slideshow about the academy, keep looking.

Two things that matter less than they seem

  • A very high rating. A 2500 grandmaster is not automatically a better teacher of six-year-olds than a 1700 player who has coached a hundred of them. Teaching is its own skill.
  • A fancy platform. Zoom plus Lichess is enough. The coach matters, not the software.
