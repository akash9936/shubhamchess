What is the right age to start chess?

By Shubham Singh, Arena International Master and FIDE rated coach. Updated 9 November 2026.

Parents ask this more than any other question. The honest answer is that there is no single right age, but there are clear stages, and a coach who teaches four-year-olds works very differently from one who teaches twelve-year-olds.

Four to six: the game as a story

At this age a child can learn how each piece moves, what check means, and the idea that the king must be kept safe. Classes are short and playful. A child who can sit with a picture book for fifteen minutes can sit with a chessboard for fifteen minutes. The goal is not to win games. It is to make the board familiar and to practise taking turns, looking before touching, and accepting a lost game calmly.

Seven to nine: the age most coaches recommend

Something changes around seven. Children start to hold a plan in mind for more than one move, they can compare two choices, and they enjoy puzzles for their own sake. This is when structured lessons pay off fastest. Most children who later play tournaments started serious lessons between seven and nine.

Ten to fifteen: never too late

Older beginners learn the rules in a week and catch up quickly, because they can read notation, study on their own, and understand why a move is good rather than only that it is good. Many strong club players began at eleven or twelve.

Signs your child is ready

  • They can follow a two-step instruction without being reminded.
  • They enjoy board games or puzzles that take more than five minutes.
  • They have asked about chess, or watched someone play and wanted to try.
  • They can lose a game of anything without the evening being ruined. Not every time, but sometimes.

What if we start and it does not stick?

Then you stop, and try again in a year. Nothing is lost. A free assessment class is the cheapest way to find out: one live session, a few puzzles, a short game, and an honest opinion on whether the time is right.
